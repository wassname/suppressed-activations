# moa-brainstorm: signed cosine agreement as a discriminator — mechanism reconstruction

## Framing: observed vs inferred

**Observed (measured):** avg-projector SVD yields sign-invariant "persistence"; removal dominates (p(6)=0.79) while donor_add alone is null (p(6)=0.01, shift 0.0); one L20 dog-property residual diff norm = 0.000 vs spider; identity not transferred ("the animal that spins webs is the spider") despite digit edits.

**Inferred (not measured):** that the missing ingredient is *signed directional consistency* of the projected component vectors c_p; that the edit is reaching the right subspace at all; that the 0.000-diff check is representative rather than a lucky single sample.

---

## Q1. Signed cosine of c_p vs subspace overlap — genuinely different?

Yes, mathematically distinct, and the distinction is testable. Subspace overlap / avg-projector spectrum answers "do B_p share a span?" Signed cos(c_p, c_q) answers "do the residuals *land on the same side* of that span?" A subspace can have high principal-angle overlap while c_p and c_q are anti-aligned or orthogonal *within* the shared span (e.g., the shared span contains a mean direction and token-specific deviations that flip sign). The avg-projector SVD is provably blind to the sign and to which direction *within* the span is occupied.

**Falsifiable prediction (distinguishes the two):** There exists a token set where avg-projector persistence is high (e.g., persistence > 0.5) but mean signed cross-token cosine is ≤ 0 (or near 0), and conversely a set where cosine is high but the shared span is low-rank-trivial (rank-1 repeat of one token). If signed cosine never decorrelates from subspace overlap across your candidate sets, the two measures carry the same information and the proposal is not a new discriminator.

**Cheapest check:** one forward pass, one sample, T=3 positions, cache B_p and c_p, compute 3 pairwise cosines + principal angles. Minutes of compute, no training.

## Q2–Q3. Candidate mechanisms (no winner)

### M1 — Implementation error: sign ambiguity in basis construction
SVD/orthonormalization returns bases with arbitrary per-vector sign; if B_p signs aren't aligned across positions before projecting, cos(c_p, c_q) measures basis-sign noise, not the residual. Conversely the *existing* pipeline could have the inverse bug: the edit direction's sign is chosen per-token, so stacking bases in the avg-projector averages away a consistent direction.
- **Prediction:** recomputing c_p with signs aligned to a reference (e.g., sign fixed by correlation with the first position, or with the removal-direction that worked) flips the cosine sign pattern.
- **Check:** recompute cosines after deterministic sign convention (largest-|·| coordinate positive). Free.

### M2 — Objective/gradient mismatch: removal ≠ addition asymmetry
The data (removal works, donor_add null, shift 0.0) suggests the training/implicit objective rewards *suppressing* the source concept, not *writing* the donor. A shared signed suppressed direction may exist but only along the "remove spider" axis; donor_add adds along a direction with no causal support (p=0.01).
- **Prediction:** cross-token signed cosine is high for the removal-relevant direction and near zero (or the projection norm is ~0) for the donor-relevant direction at the answer position.
- **Check:** compute cos/‖c_p‖ separately for source-basis vs donor-basis projections, same single forward pass.

### M3 — Unintended learning dynamic: identity resides outside the property subspace
"Spider→dog" transfers the property ("spins webs" removed) but the identity token regenerates. The 0.000-diff L20 residual is consistent with the edit being *identity-preserving by construction* — the intervention touches the property pair, and identity is carried in a different subspace/position that the last-3 prefill window doesn't cover. The null donor_add plus unchanged identity is exactly what you'd expect if the edit is a deletion, not a concept injection.
- **Prediction:** if you ablate the identity-carrying token position (not in the last-3 window) along the suppressed basis, identity transfer changes; if you ablate only the property positions, it doesn't.
- **Check:** single-token-position sweep of the intervention (one forward + 32-token gen per position, ~T runs) rather than the full window.

### M4 — Shared-mean / collapsed-latent artifact (the high-cosine false positive)
All residual streams at adjacent positions share a large common component (positional/attentional mean, sink token). If c_p is dominated by this, cross-token cosine will be high regardless of concept. Also a single dominating token can drive the average.
- **Prediction:** removing the layer mean (or the first token's contribution) collapses the cosine; the semantic signal, if any, survives in the residualized c_p.
- **Check:** recompute cosines after subtracting the mean c̄ over positions — near-free arithmetic on cached vectors.

## Q4. Cheaper/more decisive measurement

Ordered by cost:
1. **Zero-training, zero-edit diagnostics first:** signed cosine vs principal angles vs ‖c_p‖ on one sample (Q1 check) + mean-subtraction control (M4). This alone falsifies or motivates the whole proposal.
2. **Donor-specific vs generic-syntax control:** compute signed cosine of c_p against (a) donor-concept direction, (b) a generic "suppress animal word" direction built from unrelated prompts. If (a) ≈ (b), the cosine is syntax, not concept.
3. **Single-position intervention sweep** (M3) — ~T generations, directly tests whether any position carries transferable identity.
4. Only then the full proposed experiment (cosine-selected basis, 32-token continuation, matched-random control). Note the matched-random control is essential: with p(6)≈0.8 achievable by removal alone, "differs from baseline" is cheap; "differs from random-subspace edit" is the actual claim.

Also split the readout: report **norm fraction** and **cosine** separately before claiming agreement — a high cosine on a negligible-norm component is vacuous.

---

## Q5. False-positive conditions for high cross-token cosine

- Shared residual mean / position encoding common to all p (M4).
- One dominating token in the window whose c_p sets the sign for all pairs.
- Generic syntax/register direction (e.g., "next token is a noun") — cosine high across any prompt of the same form; testable via the donor-vs-generic control.
- Suppression "sink" artifact: attention-sink directions are known to be shared and large; a high cosine there says nothing about the concept.
- Sign convention artifact (M1): alignment imposed by construction, not by the model.

## Bottom line

The signed-cosine measurement is cheap and worth running as a *diagnostic*, but as specified it conflates four things (sign convention, mean sharing, norm, concept). Run the zero-edit diagnostics and the single-position sweep before investing in the full basis-selection comparison; the removal-dominance asymmetry (M2) and the identity-outside-window hypothesis (M3) are the stronger competing explanations for the null transfer, and both are testable with fewer compute than the proposed experiment. Uncertainty: all of this rests on one sample's 0.000-diff check and one config's numbers; replication across 2–3 samples is the first thing any of these checks should include.
