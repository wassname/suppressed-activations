## M1. Answer-token direction vs. belief/identity direction (basis objective mismatch)

**Mechanism.** The attenuation basis selects directions that *decay* from peak to output (`E = peakᵀpeak − finalᵀfinal`, top positive eigenvectors). These are directions whose job is finished by the output layer — plausibly the "next-token label" signal that fixes the digit/name at the answer position. Sustained identity in the description is carried by directions that *persist or rise* toward the output. Under this reading the intervention correctly flips the first token (observed in all cases, including naming) but never installs durable identity; the model then notices the mismatch between the injected label and the unpatched upstream context ("spins webs") and its conflict-resolution circuit produces self-correction — exactly the L25 loop mode, and exactly the naming-prompt failure mode.

**Falsifiable prediction.** A basis built from the opposite contrast (directions that *grow* into the final layer: `finalᵀfinal − peakᵀpeak`, or intersection-nullspace directions) should transfer the *description and naming* while possibly losing the first-token flip. Under M1, no value of C on the current basis produces coherent naming transfer.

**Cheapest check.** One run per animal: same band config, sign-flipped E (take most negative eigenvectors), naming prompts. Cost: refit U (cheap, cached residuals) + two generations. Also: sweep C ∈ {2, 4, 8} on the *current* basis for naming prompts — if output degrades into gibberish/looping before transfer, that supports direction-misalignment over insufficient strength.

**Mechanism.** The C2 injection is incoherent with the rest of the context (source prompt still says "spins webs"; the description positions are unpatched).
