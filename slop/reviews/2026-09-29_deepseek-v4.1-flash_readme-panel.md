## First call answer

The README’s central claim is narrower than the title suggests. It reports a method for isolating one kind of “hidden” representation: English intermediate content that appears in a model’s residual stream during German-to-Chinese translation but is not output. The author frames this as testing whether Gurnee-style “suppression neurons” can be found in the residual stream, using the Wendler et al. logit-lens picture—English rises in middle layers, then falls, while Chinese rises later.

The mechanism, as stated, is a “suppressed activation subspace.” At the last prompt token, the residual stream `h` is unembedded at early, peak, and output layers (22, 27, 32). The method computes a centered rise score (`z_peak - z_early`) and fall score (`z_peak - z_output`), takes the minimum of their positive parts, and selects top-32 tokens. It forms an orthogonal basis `S` from the centered unembedding rows of those tokens, then projects `h` into `h @ S @ S.T`. The evaluated object is a top-32 token set: it “passes” if the English word is present and Chinese and German are absent.

Observed results: rise-and-fall passes 56/120 German-to-Chinese prompts; plain logit lens passes 4/120. Most failures include the German input word. Causal replacement changes output on 16/50 fixed pairs, versus 0/50 random; using Chinese answer-token directions works better, 41/50. The spider-to-dog demo is hand-picked and does not establish semantic replacement. A separate tuned dog/ant method scores 6/12 fresh successes versus 1/12 random.
