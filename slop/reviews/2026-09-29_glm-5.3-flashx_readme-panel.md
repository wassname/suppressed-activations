## Central claim and mechanism

**Claim (in my own words):** When a model translates a word (e.g., German→Chinese), it briefly represents an intermediate English word in its activations that it then *suppresses* before outputting the target language. This "thought but not spoken" English leaves a characteristic signature in the residual stream — a logit that rises in middle layers and falls before the output — and the author can isolate the subspace carrying it.

**Mechanism:** At the last prompt token, unembed the residual stream at three layers (early ~22, peak ~27, output ~32). Compute `rise = peak − early` and `fall = peak − output` over vocabulary logits, then score each token by `min(relu(rise), relu(fall))` — a token must both rise *and* fall. The top-32 such tokens define a subspace (orthogonal basis of their unembedding rows), and the residual component in that subspace is claimed to be the hidden English. They then test it two ways: (1) does the top-32 token list contain the English answer but neither the Chinese (said) nor German (input) word, and (2) causally, does replacing this component with another word's component change the output?
