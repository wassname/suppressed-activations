## Q1. A statistic that genuinely separates "shared signed direction" from "orthogonal nuisance + shared mean"

The problem with every statistic used so far is that averaging over positions mixes the per-position basis **direction** (which differs) with the residual **norm** (which can be shared). Two candidates:

**(a) Top singular value of the cross-position component matrix, with an exact orthogonal+norm-matched null.**
Form the 3×T matrix C whose entries are c_p across positions *within one prompt*, or better: for each pair of tokens (source vs donor), form the matrix of c_p values at matched positions. Compute the top singular value of the mean-centered, **norm-standardized** matrix (divide each c_p by its own norm, so the statistic is pure direction). Null: generate 10k draws where each position's component is a random unit vector in its own per-position basis B_p (preserving each basis's own spectrum). This null is *stronger* than the matched-random control because it respects each basis's structure.

- Refutes the proposal: observed σ₁ within the null's 95% band at peak.
- Supports it: σ₁ significantly above null **and** the top singular vector is stable across an independent resample (e.g., split the continuation set, or use dog vs ant as two quasi-replicates — agreement must appear in *both* donor comparisons with similar singular vectors, not just one).

**(b) Cross-token signed cosine at matched positions with token-label permutation null.**
For source-vs-donor at position p: cos(c_p^source, c_p^donor) — raw, uncentered, no averaging over positions until after inspection. Null: permute position labels within token, so "agreement" must be position-locked to survive. The permutation null is the key addition: a genuine concept direction should be position-locked (same token slot, same direction); a shared-mean artifact is not.

- Prediction if real: cos > 0 at concept-bearing positions, near zero under position-permutation.
- Cheapest check: this is a handful of dot products on saved trajectories — essentially free once the bank exists.

The peak-vs-early *difference* in (a) or (b) is the cleanest single scalar, since each layer serves as its own baseline structure.

## Q2. Measurement layer, and the circularity question

**Peak (L25) is the only layer where the detector's selection is meaningful.** The tokens were selected *because* they rise to 25; measuring at 23 measures pre-rise residual content that the selection rule never certified. This is not a subtle point — at L23 you are testing a different hypothesis with tokens chosen for a different one.

**Circularity — yes, it is partially circular, and here is the exact loop:** agreement at peak could be a property of *the selection rule itself* — tokens rising into a shared subspace (e.g., a dominant "suppression mode" of the residual stream that many tokens project into at mid-depth) will show cross-token agreement at 25 by construction, regardless of suppression semantics. The rise-fall criterion guarantees you're looking where variance concentrates.

**Non-circular control (cheap):** compute the same peak-layer agreement for (i) tokens selected by a **mirrored rule** — fall-then-rise, or monotone-rise-only tokens — and (ii) top-magnitude tokens with no trajectory criterion. If agreement at peak is comparable for the mirrored/magnitude selectors, peak agreement is a property of mid-layer geometry, not the suppression detector. Also: agreement must be **concept-specific** (source-vs-donor > source-vs-same-format-neutral-control by a margin exceeding the orthogonal null). Source≈control kills the concept claim even if source beats the orthogonal null.

**What peak-vs-early increase would show:** that agreement emerges coincident with the detector's rise — consistent with a suppression state that is token-specific early and shared at peak. **What it would not show:** that this shared state is about "8 legs"/spider content, that it's causal, or that it isn't a generic mid-network mode.
