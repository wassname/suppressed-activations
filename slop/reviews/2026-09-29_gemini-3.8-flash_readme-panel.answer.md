### Central Claim and Mechanism

The document claims that intermediate representations ("thoughts") that a model activates but does not output can be isolated without using language-specific heuristics. Specifically, during non-English tasks (e.g., German-to-Chinese translation), the model momentarily activates English concepts before suppressing them in favor of the target language.

**Observed Mechanism:**
1. Residual stream activations ($h$) at the final prompt token are projected into vocabulary space across three stages: early (layer 22), peak (layer 27), and output (layer 32).
2. The method scores candidate suppressed tokens by taking the minimum of the centered positive increase from early-to-peak and decrease from peak-to-output: $\min(\text{ReLU}(\text{rise}), \text{ReLU}(\text{fall}))$.
3. The top 32 highest-scoring tokens form an orthogonal projection matrix $S$ built from their centered unembedding vectors.
4. Activations are projected onto this subspace ($h_{\text{suppressed}} = h S S^T$) to isolate suppressed concepts or intervene via vector replacement.

### Ambiguities, Unstated Facts, and Missing Reproduction Details

- **Hyperparameter Selection:** The layers (22, 27, 32) are manually hard-coded based on the observed English trajectory. The author explicitly notes that a generalized, fixed rule for layer selection across other tasks or architectures is missing.
- **Intervention Validity:** Causal patching results are fragile. For the "spider to dog" replacement, $C=1$ fails, while $C=4$ distorts 72% of the residual norm and is matched by random noise or unrelated inputs (e.g., `2 + 2`). The text concedes this fails to prove semantic replacement.
- **Missing Dataset Specifications:** While repository scripts mention `epfl-dlab/llm-latent-language`, the exact 120 German-to-Chinese prompts and 50 fixed word pairs used for benchmarking are not enumerated in the README.
- **Tuned Continuation Details:** The full continuation experiment relies on a separate "template-contrast attenuation basis" rather than the described main rise-and-fall method, leaving its underlying mathematical formula unstated.

### Three Direct Answers

1. **The challenge:** Extract words a language model internally thinks but does not output using activations alone, even when the prompt, thought, and output share the same language.
2. **Success rate and main failure:** The method succeeds on 56 of 120 prompts (46.7%), failing primarily because it captures unsaid prompt tokens (the German input word) alongside the hidden thought.
3. **Hardest section:** The "Are suppressed activations causal?" section is the hardest to interpret because the reported intervention requires extreme scaling ($C=4$), duplicates the effect with unrelated controls, and contradicts its own premise by leaving the readout spider-related.
