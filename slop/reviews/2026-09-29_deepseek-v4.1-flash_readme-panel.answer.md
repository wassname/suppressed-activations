The README’s central claim is narrower than the title suggests. It reports a method for isolating one kind of “hidden” representation: English intermediate content that appears in a model’s residual stream during German-to-Chinese translation but is not output. The author frames this as testing whether Gurnee-style “suppression neurons” can be found in the residual stream, using the Wendler et al. logit-lens picture—English rises in middle layers, then falls, while Chinese rises later.

The mechanism, as stated, is a “suppressed activation subspace.” At the last prompt token, the residual stream `h` is unembedded at early, peak, and output layers (22, 27, 32). The method computes a centered rise score (`z_peak - z_early`) and fall score (`z_peak - z_output`), takes the minimum of their positive parts, and selects top-32 tokens. It forms an orthogonal basis `S` from the centered unembedding rows of those tokens, then projects `h` into `h @ S @ S.T`. The evaluated object is a top-32 token set: it “passes” if the English word is present and Chinese and German are absent.

Observed results: rise-and-fall passes 56/120 German-to-Chinese prompts; plain logit lens passes 4/120. Most failures include the German input word. Causal replacement changes output on 16/50 fixed pairs, versus 0/50 random; using Chinese answer-token directions works better, 41/50. The spider-to-dog demo is hand-picked and does not establish semantic replacement. A separate tuned dog/ant method scores 6/12 fresh successes versus 1/12 random.

Ambiguities and missing facts are substantial. The operational meaning of “thinks but does not say” is not fully defined; the pass criterion depends on known English/German/Chinese answer keys and exact token matching. The all-English challenge is posed but not tested. Layer choice is taken from the English curve, which the “Next” section itself says should become a fixed rule. The claim that the method “may not use language” is unclear because it uses unembedding vocabulary and selected tokens. The README title is incomplete. The exact model, tokenizer, prompt construction, centering operation, `orthogonal_basis`, seeds, and dependencies are not in the README. Reproduction requires scripts, external word lists, notebooks, run reports, and likely GPU setup. The text does not support robust causal editing, semantic spider→dog replacement, all-English success, or a general detector of hidden thought. It also warns that an earlier “95% English inside the subspace” figure was circular and dropped.

1. The challenge is to find words a model thinks but does not say using only activations, even when input, thought, and output are all English, without using language.

2. The rise-and-fall method passes 56/120 German-to-Chinese prompts; its main failure is including the German input word, while causal edits work only 16/50 and Chinese directions work better.

3. The hardest part is the causal-intervention caveat: at `C=4` it extrapolates past the replacement, changes 72% of residual norm, and does not establish semantic `spider → dog` replacement.
